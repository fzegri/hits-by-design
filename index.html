Project description

This project uses Six Sigma Yellow Belt DMAIC tools and a streaming dashboard to answer one question: how should Omar Courtz build and time his third album, based on how the songs on his first two albums performed. It treats an album as a product line. Each song is a product, and its daily Spotify streams are the quality measure.

Author: Fernando Zegrí, University of Puerto Rico. Built as a Six Sigma Yellow Belt portfolio project. Live site: fzegri.github.io/hits-by-design · Source code: github.com/fzegri/hits-by-design

    Limitations
    • Small sample: 35 songs and 2 albums. Patterns are labeled by whether both albums agree. They're associations, not proven causes.
    • Short history: weekly snapshots started Oct 31, 2026 ( so far). Trends get more reliable as weeks build up.
    • Not measured: song quality, promotion budget, playlist placement and music videos aren't in public data. They likely explain much of the difference between songs.
    • Release date: based on patterns from 2 past albums. It doesn't account for competing releases or seasonality.

    Read with care
    • 35 songs, 2 albums. These patterns are associations to test, not causes. Medians are used because one song (¿Qué Vas a Hacer Hoy?, 714M) skews every average.
    • Time out matters. Primera Musa has had days, Por Si Mañana . Compare using current daily or per day since release, not totals.
    • Release dates come from Apple Music and Wikipedia: 6 pre-release singles, 1 album-eve single (Goddess) and 1 post-album single (¿Qué Vas a Hacer Hoy?). "Per day since release" uses each song's own release date.
    • Cleaning applied: stylized titles normalized (Spotify spelling kept in SpotifyTitle). Tito El Bambino removed from $uelta Gatita $uelta (not in official credits). Myke Towers on What U Need? verified. Producers (KARBeats, BASSYY) are not counted as features. Duration bands added. Release type filled in for all songs.

      C

      Control

      How to keep the gains and spot problems early

      Momentum ratio: is each song gaining or fading?

      Ratio = current daily streams ÷ average streams per day since release. Above 1 means the song is doing better now than its own average (growing). Below 1 means it's fading. Per-day figures use each song's own release date.

      WhatHowTrigger → action

      One point per weekly snapshot. The dashed line for each album is the median of its points, the standard run-chart center line. Watch for runs of points on one side of it, or sudden jumps.

      Median momentum ratio of Por Si Mañana's songs at each snapshot. When it stays below the 0.8 line for 4 weeks in a row, start the Album 3 singles.
